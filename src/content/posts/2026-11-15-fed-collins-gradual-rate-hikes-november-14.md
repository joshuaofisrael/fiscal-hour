Boston Federal Reserve President Susan Collins said on Saturday that she favours a steady pace of modest interest rate increases, arguing that it better manages the uncertainties facing the US economy. "A steady, gradual path seems appropriate to me at the moment. But I'm open-minded," she told reporters at a Boston Fed conference on housing, [according to a Reuters report carried by CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/feds-collins-says-gradual-rate-hikes-best-manage-economic-risks-6552151).

## Two-sided risks

Collins said she sees the risks to the economy as two-sided: inflation is high and spreading more widely, while the job market is stable and could pick up. She also flagged the chance of further shocks and said the rise in medium and longer-term interest rates since the start of the year could weigh on demand more than she expects.

Taken together, she said, those factors argue against the Fed moving to a more aggressive pace of rate rises. Inflation has been above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years, Reuters noted.

## A higher path

Collins added that gradual moves do not necessarily mean a set cadence. She said the price data from the past three months and a firmer labour market have led her to think the path of policy rates over the next year might be somewhat higher than she expected a couple of months ago.

## What markets expect

The Fed last raised rates in September. It is widely expected to lift its current 3.75% to 4% target range by a quarter point at its 8 to 9 December meeting and again in January, Reuters reported, though last week's price data and comments from Governor Christopher Waller have suggested some officials may want a bigger move next month.

Longer-term borrowing costs have already climbed. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 0.294 percentage point over the past week to 5.152% on Friday, [according to Dow Jones Market Data via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611139683/10-year-treasury-yield-rises-to-5152-this-week-data-talk).
