US producer prices rose 0.3% in October on a seasonally adjusted basis, the [Bureau of Labor Statistics reported](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm) on Friday. That was a little softer than the 0.4% economists expected, [according to Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-november-13-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-ppi-141451558.html), but the yearly figure was not.

## The annual rate

Before seasonal adjustment, the index for final demand rose 6.5% in the 12 months to October, the BLS said. That is the largest 12-month increase since November 2022, when it rose 7.4%. Prices had risen 1.4% in September alone, according to the BLS.

## Goods and services

The BLS said prices for final demand goods rose 0.5% in October and final demand services rose 0.2%. Over the year, final demand goods were up 10.2%. Within goods, food prices rose 1.3% for the month while energy slipped 0.1%, with diesel fuel down 7.9%. Basic organic chemicals rose 3.9%.

On the services side, truck transportation of freight rose 3.9%, the BLS said.

A core measure that strips out food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% in October and 4.9% over 12 months.

## Market reaction

The 10-year Treasury yield rose nearly 4 basis points to 5.14% after the data, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Yahoo Finance said the annual rate highlighted persistent inflation pressures that likely keep the Federal Reserve on track to raise rates in December.

The next PPI release, covering November, is scheduled for 15 December, according to the BLS.

## Analysis

A softer monthly figure did little to change the picture of strong price pressure at the wholesale level, with goods prices up more than 10% on the year. Producer prices can feed through to consumer prices with a lag, which is why markets read this report alongside the Fed's next decision. This article is news, not investment advice.
