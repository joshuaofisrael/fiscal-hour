The question on Wall Street this week is simple: is this a dip to buy, or the start of something worse? "Correction or deeper reckoning? Investors seek signs US stock selling will abate," [Reuters posted on X](https://x.com/Reuters/status/2124481808188227972) on Sunday.

## How far stocks have fallen

According to the [Reuters analysis carried by Livemint](https://www.livemint.com/market/correction-or-deeper-reckoning-investors-seek-signs-us-stock-selling-will-abate-11795348893667.html), the S&P 500 has dropped 11.6% from its 6 October high. That is its first correction, a fall of 10% or more, since March 2025, and it came with seven losing sessions in a row. The Nasdaq is now down 2.7% for 2026.

Fear has jumped. The VIX, Wall Street's volatility gauge, closed on Friday at 35.89, Reuters reports. The seven-day losing run is the S&P 500's longest since the start of the pandemic in 2020.

## What investors are watching

Reuters says strategists are looking for a few signs that selling is running out of steam:

- Market internals, such as how many stocks sit at 52-week lows and how few trade above their 50-day averages
- Strength in economically sensitive groups such as banks and industrials, not just defensive stocks
- The VIX, which closed at its highest level since the April 2025 tariff turmoil

The calendar matters too. The Federal Reserve meets on 8-9 December, and Reuters says markets expect a quarter-point rate rise. Micron, a key read on AI chip demand, reports on 17 December. This week is shortened by Thanksgiving, which can mean thin trading and sharper moves.

## The split on the Street

Reuters quotes strategists on both sides. "We are getting close to a great dip-buying point," said Bruce Zaro of Granite Wealth Management. Glenmede's Michael Reynolds said the VIX was nearing the 40 level that could indicate "peak fear". Edward Jones's Angelo Kourkafas said that when the VIX has closed above 30 for the first time in a cycle, the S&P 500 has averaged an 8.1% total return six months later.

Others are less sure. "This seems like a real correction which has further to go," said Tim Ghriskey of Ingalls & Snyder. State Street's Michael Arone said investors fear the Fed "is tightening into an economic slowdown".

## What it means for you

Corrections are common, but they are uncomfortable, and no one knows in advance which ones become bear markets. For long-term investors, the key dates are the Fed meeting and Micron's results. This is market news, not investment advice.
