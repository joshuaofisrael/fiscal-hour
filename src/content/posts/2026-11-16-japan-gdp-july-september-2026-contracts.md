Japan's economy contracted slightly in the July to September quarter. Real gross domestic product fell 0.02% from the previous quarter, an annualised fall of 0.1%, the Cabinet Office said in its [first preliminary estimate](https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/jp/sna/data/data_list/sokuhou/files/2026/qe263/pdf/qepoint2631.pdf) on Monday. It was the first contraction in four quarters.

Economists had expected growth. [Jiji Press](https://jen.jiji.com/business/economy/2026111600172/) says the median forecast among 21 think tanks it surveyed was a 0.3% annualised rise, and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/japan-q3-gdp-energy-oil-prices.html) cites a Reuters poll with the same figure. In nominal terms GDP rose 0.4% on the quarter, or 1.6% annualised.

## Where the weakness came from

The Cabinet Office says private consumption fell 0.3% in real terms, a second straight quarterly decline, with spending on items such as air conditioners and daily goods lower. Housing investment fell 1.1%. Net exports subtracted 0.6 percentage point from growth as imports rose 2.5%, led by crude oil, while exports slipped 0.2%. Domestic demand added 0.6 point.

Business investment was the bright spot, rising 1.6% on spending on computers such as servers and on chipmaking equipment, according to the [Cabinet Office](https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/en/sna/data/sokuhou/files/2026/qe263/pdf/gaiyou2631_e.pdf).

CNBC reports that household energy subsidies expired in September, leaving consumers exposed to higher energy prices linked to the Iran war. Compared with a year earlier, the economy grew 1.1%, helped by a weak base in the third quarter of 2025.

## What it means for the Bank of Japan

The data lands weeks before the Bank of Japan's December meeting. CNBC says markets still priced a 75.6% chance of a quarter-point rate rise in December, according to LSEG. State Street Investment Management economist Krishna Bhimavarapu told CNBC the figures were "hardly a green light" for a hike, pointing to a 2.1% annualised fall in real final sales of domestic product. Capital Economics still expects the central bank to tighten.

After the release the Nikkei 225 fell 0.62% and the yen traded around 159.76 per dollar, CNBC reports. The 16 November story reported this as the first estimate, with a revision expected once corporate investment surveys were in. Checked on 21 November, the figures above are still that first preliminary estimate. A later Cabinet Office file was not the source for this update.

## Background

The contraction sits next to two decisions already reported here. On 30 October the [Bank of Japan kept the overnight call rate](/articles/2026-10-30-boj-holds-call-rate/) around 1.25%, which is the policy setting this GDP print arrived into. The US figure for a similar quarter is the [BEA advance estimate](/articles/2026-10-30-gdp-advance-third-quarter/), a different economy and a different agency. The next US release dates are on the [calendar](/calendar/). CNBC’s December hike probability, cited above, is a market price from 16 November, not a Bank of Japan announcement.
