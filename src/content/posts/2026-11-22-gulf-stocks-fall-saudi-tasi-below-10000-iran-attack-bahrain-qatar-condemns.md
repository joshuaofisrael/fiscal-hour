Gulf investors started the week selling. "Most Gulf bourses retreat as sporadic attacks continue," [Reuters posted on X](https://x.com/Reuters/status/2124483068459176257) on Sunday. Earlier in the day, [First Squawk posted](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124477529436414252) that Qatar condemned an "Iranian attack on civilian industrial facility in Bahrain."

## The numbers

According to [Forbes Middle East's closing report](https://forbesmiddleeast.com/money/markets/gulf-stock-markets-retreat-amid-regional-tensions-while-kuwait-bucks-the-trend), Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All Share Index fell 1.95% to 9,960.44, slipping under the 10,000 mark. Decliners beat gainers by 238 to 29. [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/closing-bell-saudi-main-index-slips-to-close-at-9960-3013541) reported the same close.

Forbes Middle East reported these other Sunday closes:

- Qatar: down 1.59% to 9,902.87
- Bahrain: down 0.56% to 1,813.39
- Oman: down 0.77%
- Kuwait: down 0.45%
- Egypt's EGX30: down 2.85%

Markets in the UAE do not trade on Sundays, so Abu Dhabi and Dubai will give their first read on Monday.

## What drove it

Forbes Middle East says Saudi Arabia's defence ministry intercepted a Houthi drone heading towards Riyadh, and the Bahrain Defence Force said it intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks. Qatar's foreign ministry condemned the attack on the Bahraini facility, according to First Squawk. Officials have not given a full public account of damage.

## Why it matters beyond the Gulf

Gulf exchanges are the first big markets to trade each week, so they are an early gauge of how investors read weekend events in the region. A broad sell-off in Riyadh and Doha points to nervousness about attacks on industrial sites, not just military targets.

The next test comes when oil trading reopens on Sunday evening and the UAE markets open on Monday. Investors will be watching whether energy prices react and whether the attacks continue. This is market news, not investment advice.
