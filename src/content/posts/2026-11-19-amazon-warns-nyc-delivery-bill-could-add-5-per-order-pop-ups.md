Amazon has taken a fight with New York City into its own shopping app. "Amazon launches in-app push against Mamdani-backed delivery bill in New York City," [CNBC posted on X](https://x.com/CNBC/status/2123451582624751675) on Thursday morning.

## What shoppers are seeing

According to [CNBC's report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/amazon-nyc-delivery-protection-act.html), some users of Amazon's app and website are being shown pop-ups and a homepage banner warning that the City Council "could add up to $5 per order" if it passes Intro 518, known as the Delivery Protection Act. The notices link to a form to contact a council member.

Amazon published the same message [on its own site](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/policy-news-views/amazon-new-york-city-delivery-protection-act-costs). "If this legislation passes, we'd no longer be able to work with them, and we'd be forced to consider moving our operations outside of the city," it says, referring to the small delivery businesses it contracts. Amazon says that "would likely mean slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers".

## What the bill would do

CNBC says the bill would require Amazon to employ the workers of its third-party delivery companies directly, disrupting the contractor model it has used since 2018. The bill was introduced in February and is awaiting review by City Council Speaker Julie Menin. Mayor Zohran Mamdani endorsed it in August, saying the subcontractor model "leaves workers vulnerable and corporations free to avoid accountability".

The $5 figure is Amazon's own estimate, labelled "up to". Neither Amazon nor CNBC published how it was calculated.

## Why it matters for the business

Last-mile delivery is one of Amazon's biggest cost lines, and the contractor network is how it scales it. CNBC notes it is rare for Amazon to use its store pages for policy campaigns, although Uber and Lyft have done the same in their apps. Labor groups including the Teamsters back the bill; business groups have warned they may sue if it passes. Amazon's latest results are in our [third-quarter coverage](/articles/2026-10-30-amazon-third-quarter-results/).

## What to watch

Whether the council schedules a vote, and whether the bill is amended. Until then, the $5 warning is a lobbying claim, not a price change.
