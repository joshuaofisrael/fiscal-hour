Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court (TSE) [results page](https://resultados.tse.jus.br/oficial/app/index.html) for the presidential runoff shows all 499,248 voting sections tallied. It lists Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva with 51.79% of valid votes (64,256,232) and Flávio Bolsonaro with 48.21% (59,826,041), as of its last update at 23:26 local time on Oct. 25. The page reports a tally; it does not by itself mark the formal certification step. [The BBC reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmvg7p001qj9o) on Monday that Lula had been re-elected and that Bolsonaro had not conceded.

Bolsonaro has refused to recognize the result, the [BBC said](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmvg7p001qj9o), and it reported that he told supporters in Brasília he would seek to have the election annulled. That is his stated position, as reported by the BBC, not a finding about the vote. FiscalHour has not seen any TSE ruling on such a challenge.

The BBC said this is Lula’s fourth presidential win. He served from January 2003 to December 2010 and again from January 2023. The new term is set to begin on Jan. 5, 2027, [the BBC reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmvg7p001qj9o). It said Bolsonaro finished ahead of Lula in the first round on Oct. 4.

The [BBC described](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmvg7p001qj9o) a difficult political map for the winner: a Congress it called the most right-wing of Brazil’s democratic era, with Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party making the largest gains, and more than 48% of voters choosing Bolsonaro. It said Lula will be 85 by the end of the term. Those are the BBC’s characterizations of the result.

On the economy, the [BBC said](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmvg7p001qj9o) Brazil is rich in oil and holds the world’s second-largest reserves of rare earth minerals, and that it is the only large South American country not led by a right-wing government. It said the White House wants Latin American governments aligned with U.S. policy on defense, security and trade, and wants less Chinese influence in the hemisphere. It said Trump did not directly endorse Flávio Bolsonaro. It also said Lula had warned the United States not to interfere and had insisted Brazil would “never fit into anyone’s backyard.”

This article does not report a price for the real, for Brazilian shares, or for any commodity. A currency or equity move belongs in a later story only with a market source that states it. The election result is a political fact with economic stakes. It is not a view on Brazilian assets.
