Canaccord Genuity Group has agreed to sell its wealth management business in the UK and Crown Dependencies to Centerbridge Partners and Mubadala Investment Company, and its chairman and chief executive Dan Daviau has agreed to take the rest of the Toronto-listed company private. The two linked deals were announced on the evening of Sunday 15 November (US Eastern time).

## The wealth sale

[Mubadala says](https://www.mubadala.com/en/news/mubadala-and-centerbridge-partners-to-acquire-canaccord-genuitys-uk-wealth-management-business) the all-cash deal values Canaccord Wealth at an enterprise value of about £1.17 billion. The business had £43.4 billion in assets under management and administration at 30 June 2026. The existing management team will keep a meaningful ownership stake.

Canaccord holds an equity interest of about 66.9% in the unit on an as-converted basis and expects to receive about £564 million after adjustments for debt, working capital, costs and timing, the company said in its [sale announcement](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/canaccord-genuity-group-inc-announces-sale-of-its-wealth-management-business-in-the-uk-amp-crown-dependencies-to-centerbridge-partners-l-p-and-mubadala-investment-company-pjsc-858120757.html). The sale needs approval from regulators including the UK Financial Conduct Authority.

## The take-private offer

Under a separate [arrangement agreement](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/canaccord-genuity-group-inc-enters-into-agreement-to-be-taken-private-by-chairman-and-ceo-dan-daviau-835103153.html), a company wholly owned by Daviau would buy all common shares he and his affiliates do not already own. Shareholders would get C$7.45 a share for the remaining business plus £4.15 a share from the wealth sale proceeds. At the exchange rate on 13 November that adds up to about C$15.27 a share, but the sterling part will be converted only at closing, so the final Canadian dollar value could be higher or lower. Holders of the Series C preferred shares would receive C$25.30 a share plus accrued dividends.

The company says a special committee of independent directors unanimously recommended the offer and the board approved it, with Daviau abstaining. Holders of about 35% of the common shares have indicated support.

## What could still change

The take-private depends on the wealth sale closing, but the sale will go ahead even if the buyout does not, Canaccord says. In that case it says there is no assurance the sale proceeds would be returned to shareholders. Both deals still need shareholder, court and regulatory approvals and are expected to close in the first half of 2027.
