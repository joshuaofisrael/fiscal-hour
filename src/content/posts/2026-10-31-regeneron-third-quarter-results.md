Regeneron Pharmaceuticals reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $5.12 billion, up 36% from $3.75 billion a year earlier, in its [results release](https://investor.regeneron.com/news-releases/news-release-details/regeneron-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-and-operating/) on Friday, 30 October. Net income under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) was $1.95 billion, or $18.45 a diluted share, up 35% per share. Non-GAAP earnings, a company measure that leaves out items such as stock-based pay, were $20.07 a share, up 70%.

Collaboration revenue from Sanofi rose 68% to $2.72 billion. Regeneron said this reflected a higher share of profits from the antibodies the two companies sell together, led by Dupixent, and the full repayment of a development balance by the end of the second quarter, which had previously reduced that revenue. Dupixent global net sales, which Sanofi records, rose 35% to $6.5 billion.

Among the products Regeneron sells itself, U.S. net sales of the eye drug EYLEA HD rose 62% to $699 million, while U.S. sales of the original EYLEA fell 41% to $400 million. Global Libtayo sales rose 51% to $550 million. The company said it had expanded its Sanofi collaboration to add several new antibodies, with a $1.0 billion up-front payment to be recognised as revenue in the fourth quarter and up to $7.0 billion more in possible milestone payments. It also declared a dividend of $0.94 a share, payable on 3 December to holders of record on 20 November.

Regeneron shares rose 13.33% to close at $927.89 on Friday, a 52-week high, [Investing.com reported](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-stocks-lower-at-close-of-trade-dow-jones-industrial-average-down-109-4988649). Chief financial officer Christopher Fenimore said in the release that the company was positioned "to deliver one of our strongest years ever". That is the company's own view.
