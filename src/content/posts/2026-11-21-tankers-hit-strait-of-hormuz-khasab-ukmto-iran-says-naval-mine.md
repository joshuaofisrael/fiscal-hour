Shipping in the world's most important oil chokepoint had a violent Saturday morning. "UKMTO Confirms Two Reported Attacks on Tankers Near Khasab, Oman, on Saturday," [First Squawk posted on X](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124087725444006058). Less than half an hour later it added a second report: "Oil Tanker Hit by Naval Mine East of Strait of Hormuz, Catches Fire, Iranian State TV Reports," [First Squawk posted](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124094794830057754).

## What UKMTO says happened

The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, which tracks attacks on merchant ships, published [Warning 215-26](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/20261121-ukmto_warning_215-26.pdf). It says a tanker "transiting outbound in the Strait of Hormuz has been hit on the stern by an unknown projectile" 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, at 05:00 UTC. "There are no reported casualties at this time. Environmental impact is currently unconfirmed," the warning says, and it tells vessels "to transit with caution."

Fifty minutes later, at 05:50 UTC, UKMTO's [Warning 216-26](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/20261121-ukmto_warning_216-26.pdf) reported a second incident: an outbound tanker hit in the engine room by an unknown projectile 17 nautical miles east of Khasab, again with no reported casualties. It was not clear whether the two reports involved the same ship, [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/two-tanker-attacks-reported-near-omans-khasab-ukmto-says.html). CNBC adds that a tanker near Khasab caught fire in an attack on Friday too.

## Iran's version

Iran's navy said an oil tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait, according to CNBC, which notes that Reuters could not verify the Iranian account or tie it to the UKMTO reports. Tehran has imposed restrictions on ships passing through the strait and has attacked vessels it says broke them, CNBC says.

## Why oil traders will be watching the reopen

Khasab sits on Oman's Musandam Peninsula at the entrance to the strait, which carried nearly one-fifth of global crude supplies before the Iran war began, according to CNBC. Repeated hits on tankers raise the cost of insuring and chartering ships for the route, and that feeds into what refiners pay for crude.

Markets were closed when the reports came in, so the first reaction will come when trading reopens. The questions to watch are whether more ships are hit, whether the projectiles are identified, and whether shipowners start avoiding the strait.
