Walmart heads into the holidays with a higher forecast. The retailer "reported earnings and revenue that narrowly topped estimates and raised its full-year outlook as it saw strong e-commerce growth ahead of the busy holiday season," [CNBC posted on X](https://x.com/CNBC/status/2123394139412713630) on Thursday morning. [Bloomberg](https://x.com/business/status/2123442464849600679) said Walmart's focus on prices and e-commerce "continue to win over shoppers across income groups".

## The quarter

According to [CNBC's report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/walmart-wmt-q3-2026-earnings.html), Walmart posted adjusted earnings of 65 cents a share against 64 cents expected, and revenue of $188.84 billion against $187.27 billion expected, based on LSEG estimates. Revenue rose 5.2%. Net income fell to $4.95 billion, or 62 cents a share, from $6.14 billion, or 77 cents, a year earlier.

Walmart U.S. net sales rose 3.6% to $125 billion, with comparable sales excluding fuel up 2.9%. Sam's Club net sales rose 10% to $25.9 billion.

## Online is doing the work

Global e-commerce sales grew 17%, with Walmart U.S. up 23% and Sam's Club U.S. up 30%, driven by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery. Walmart U.S. advertising sales rose 37%. Walmart said it rolled back prices on nearly 8,000 products.

## The new guidance

Walmart now expects full-year net sales to rise 4.9% to 5.2%, up from 4% to 5%. It sees adjusted earnings per share of $2.84 to $2.89, up from $2.80 to $2.87, and adjusted operating income growth of 8% to 8.6%, up from 7% to 8.5%.

## What the bosses said

Chief executive John Furner told CNBC diesel costs have been higher than expected, and that tariff refunds have helped offset them. "We're hoping to see these costs come down as soon as possible, and we'll keep prices as low as we can," he said. He also said Walmart's AI shopping agent, Sparky, has led to customer basket increases of 40% on average. That is the company's figure; Walmart did not say how it was measured.

Chief financial officer John David Rainey said the fourth quarter is "off to a pretty good start". CNBC says the shares are up roughly 13% this year.
