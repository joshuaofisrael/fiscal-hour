One of Wall Street's best-known bears has a blunt theory about what it will take to make the Federal Reserve blink. "Quickest Way To Stop The Fed: Hartnett Says Stocks Must Give Up All 2026's Gains, And S&P Is 80 Points Away," [zerohedge posted on X](https://x.com/zerohedge/status/2124314536446411208) on Saturday night.

## What Hartnett is arguing

Michael Hartnett is chief investment strategist at Bank of America. In its [write-up of his latest note](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/quickest-way-stop-fed-hartnett-says-stocks-must-give-all-2026s-gains-and-sp-80-points-away), ZeroHedge says the "fastest cure" for an inflationary, K-shaped AI boom is "lower stocks and wider credit spreads." In other words, a market slide that tightens financial conditions could do the Fed's job for it.

ZeroHedge says Hartnett stayed risk-off a week ago, when the S&P 500 was at 7,562, and that his bear case began with the index below 7,000. The "80 points" figure in the headline is ZeroHedge's reading of how far the index is from wiping out its 2026 gain. FiscalHour has not independently checked that gap.

## Where the market actually is

The backdrop is rough. A [Reuters analysis carried by Livemint](https://www.livemint.com/market/correction-or-deeper-reckoning-investors-seek-signs-us-stock-selling-will-abate-11795348893667.html) says the S&P 500 is down 11.6% from its 6 October high after seven straight losing sessions, its first correction since March 2025. The Nasdaq is down 2.7% for the year, and the VIX volatility index closed on Friday at 35.89.

Reuters reports that markets expect the Fed to raise rates by a quarter point at its 8-9 December meeting. That is the hike Hartnett's logic says a deeper sell-off could head off.

## What to watch

The coming week is shortened by Thanksgiving, so moves on thin volume can be exaggerated. If the S&P 500 does give up its full-year gain, Hartnett's framework says the pressure shifts to the Fed. If stocks steady, December's hike stays on the table. This is one strategist's view, not a forecast of what the Fed will do, and nothing here is investment advice.
