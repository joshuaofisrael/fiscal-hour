Confidence among US builders of new single-family homes fell four points to 25 in November, according to the NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index [released on Tuesday](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2026/11/builder-sentiment-continues-to-fall-on-affordability-concerns). That is the lowest reading since April 2012. Any figure below 50 means more builders see conditions as poor than good.

## All three parts fell

The index of current sales conditions fell six points to 28, the gauge of sales expectations for the next six months dropped two points to 30, and the measure of prospective buyer traffic fell two points to 16. The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) says that traffic reading is the lowest in 15 years, leaving aside the start of the pandemic in 2020.

## Price cuts and incentives

In November, 46% of builders cut prices, the highest share since the pandemic, and the average cut held at 6% for an eighth straight month. Sixty-eight per cent offered sales incentives, also a post-Covid high, and the 20th month in a row in which at least 60% did so.

## What builders blame

NAHB chairman Bill Owens said stepped-up immigration enforcement "is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs", and that higher fuel prices are pushing subcontractors to raise their rates sharply. Chief economist Robert Dietz pointed to material costs pushed up by tariffs, worker shortages and regulation, adding that "mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back."

## Regional picture

On a three-month moving average, the Northeast fell four points to 34, the Midwest five points to 35, the South three points to 27 and the West three points to 23.

## Why it matters

Owens also said that with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction. With nearly half of builders already cutting prices and buyer traffic at a 15-year low, that could mean fewer new homes started in the months ahead, at a time when the industry says affordability is already stretched.
