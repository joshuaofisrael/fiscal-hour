The AI boom has a new critic, and it sits at the top of the Federal Reserve. "Fed's Jefferson warns AI investment boom is fueling inflation," [Walter Bloomberg posted on X](https://x.com/DeItaone/status/2123422477615280450) on Thursday morning. Hours later [First Squawk posted](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2123519098059640843) that Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee "does not see inflation on the path back to 2%, with price pressures coming not just from oil and tariff shocks but also a sizable demand component."

## What Jefferson said

The Fed's vice chair spoke at a conference in Brasília. In the [published text](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/jefferson20261119a.htm) he calls rapid AI adoption a shock that hits demand and supply at different times. Optimism about the technology "has been boosting business investment in data centers, advanced computing equipment, and related products," he said. "Currently, the AI buildout is exerting upward pressure on demand and inflation."

The upside comes later. "In the future, advancements in productivity could exert downward pressure on inflation while continuing to support growth," Jefferson said. He also warned that "AI could cause some job displacement, a meaningful challenge for affected households." He gave no signal about the next rate decision.

## What Goolsbee said

Goolsbee went further. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-boom-inflation-chicago-fed-goolsbee/) quotes him saying "the evidence does not suggest we are on a path back to 2%" and calling inflation "borderline disturbingly high." CBS notes that he becomes a voting member of the rate-setting committee in January.

## Why this matters

For two years the AI spending wave has been treated mostly as a boost to growth. Two senior officials now say it is also feeding inflation, and that strengthens the case for higher rates. The October minutes already showed officials discussing a hike, as covered in our [minutes story](/articles/2026-11-19-fomc-minutes-october-2026-rate-hike-ai-debt-financial-stability/). Boston Fed President Susan Collins made the case for gradual increases last week, as we reported in our [Collins story](/articles/2026-11-15-fed-collins-gradual-rate-hikes-november-14/).

## What to watch

The Fed meets in December. Any further comments from officials on AI spending, together with the next inflation data, will show whether a hike is getting closer.
