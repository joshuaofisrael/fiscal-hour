The Federal Reserve kept rates on hold in October, but the minutes of that meeting show a committee leaning firmly towards tightening. The Fed [posted the minutes on X](https://x.com/federalreserve/status/2123129448644190523) on Wednesday afternoon.

## The decision and the dissents

According to the [minutes of the 27 to 28 October meeting](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm), the Federal Open Market Committee kept the target range for the federal funds rate at 3.75% to 4% by a 10 to 2 vote. Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack dissented, preferring a quarter-point increase.

Most participants backed holding, partly to see how September's increase was feeding through and how durable AI-related investment demand would be. A few of them called it a close call. Some participants wanted a 25 basis point rise at the meeting, saying inflation was too high and showing few signs of easing.

## The signal for December

The key line for markets: almost all participants judged that further increases in the target range would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks. A couple said a move of more than 25 basis points could deserve consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable. Many stressed that holding in October did not signal any weaker commitment to getting inflation back to 2%.

The New York Fed's market desk told the meeting that futures prices gave high probabilities to a quarter-point hike by year-end and two more by summer 2027.

## Inflation and the AI buildout

Staff estimated that PCE inflation rose to 3.6% in September and core PCE to 3.1%. Participants said price pressures were broad based. Many described the AI buildout as a material source of inflation risk, with demand for related products straining supply and raising prices of technology consumer goods, and several judged those pressures were spreading beyond industries tied to data centre construction.

## AI debt and financial stability

In the financial stability discussion, almost all participants talked about the risk that returns on AI investment disappoint investors. Most observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly, much of it through complex and opaque financing structures, and a few said AI exposures were becoming concentrated in a small number of firms. Staff judged the banking system well capitalised, but several participants warned that bank vulnerabilities can emerge quickly and that supervisors may not fully see banks' indirect exposure through lenders such as private credit funds.

## Why it matters

The minutes describe an economy growing strongly, with inflation above target for a long stretch and officials worried about expectations becoming entrenched. That makes December a live meeting.
