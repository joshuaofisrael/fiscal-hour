Lending against a GPU is easy until the GPU might end up in orbit. "SpaceX's, $SPCX, lenders want to stop it from launching $40 billion worth of AI chips into space, according to the Financial Times," [Unusual Whales posted on X](https://x.com/unusual_whales/status/2123757500570931417) on Friday morning.

## How the deal works

The post explains the set-up: an Apollo-managed entity buys Nvidia GPUs and leases them to SpaceX, which repays over about five years. "If SpaceX defaults, the lenders can take back the chips and sell them," it says. "That's the problem with space." Lenders worry they could not reclaim chips sent to orbiting data centres, so they "are now demanding legal protections to keep the chips on the ground."

[Investing.com's write-up](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-lenders-seek-safeguards-against-musk-sending-ai-chips-into-space--ft-5040555) of the FT report puts the debt package at $43 billion and calls it Project Saturn. It says the financing includes a $25 billion investment-grade bond backed by the chip lease payments, priced at about 8.3% and rated A3 by Moody's and A-minus by KBRA, citing Bloomberg.

## Two details that matter

First, it is unclear whether the restriction made it into the final loan documents, according to the FT as reported by Investing.com. Second, SpaceX has said the chips will be used in data centres on Earth. The lenders' concern is about what could happen later, not current plans.

The X post adds that the deal closed in October with leases starting in the first half of 2027, and quotes Musk saying debt markets have "almost entirely shut off access to money" for AI companies without other big revenue streams. We could not find a separate source for those points this morning, so treat them as the poster's.

## Why it matters

Chip-backed lending has become a major way to pay for AI data centres. Regulators are already watching it, as covered in our [story on Fed Governor Barr's warning about data-centre debt](/articles/2026-11-17-fed-barr-ai-financial-stability-data-centre-debt/). A lender asking where its collateral will physically sit is a reminder that GPUs lose value quickly and are only worth something if someone can get them back.
