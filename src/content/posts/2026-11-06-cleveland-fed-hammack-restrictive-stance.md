Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said on 5 November 2026 that she sees inflation as relatively broad based and that she expects policy will need to move to a restrictive stance to bring inflation back to 2 percent in a timely manner.

The [Cleveland Fed's X account](https://x.com/ClevelandFed/status/2118345099838951524) quoted those lines and linked her full remarks from The SNB and Its Watchers Conference in Zurich. The speech, titled [Reserve Currencies and Stablecoins](https://www.clevelandfed.org/collections/speeches/2026/sp-20261105-reserve-currencies-and-stablecoins), carries her usual disclaimer that the views are hers and not those of the Federal Reserve System or her FOMC colleagues.

## What the speech adds

In the prepared text, Hammack said that since she spoke on related themes in early March, inflation has moved the wrong way. She cited headline PCE inflation at 3.6 percent in September and core PCE at 3.1 percent, both well above the 2 percent goal and higher than at the start of the year. She said inflation has now been above goal for nearly six years, with pressures that look relatively broad based across supply and demand.

On the path for policy, she wrote that she expects a move to a restrictive stance and that she prefers getting there through more frequent smaller adjustments when possible, which she described as less disruptive for households, businesses, and markets. The Zurich talk also covered the dollar's reserve role, Swiss franc safe haven dynamics, and her skepticism that volatile cryptocurrencies threaten the dollar, while flagging dollar pegged stablecoins as a channel she is watching.

## How to read it

Hammack is one regional Fed president. Her call for a restrictive stance is a policy preference expressed in a conference speech, not an FOMC decision. Markets often treat such remarks as colour on the debate over how tight policy needs to be while inflation sits above 2 percent.
