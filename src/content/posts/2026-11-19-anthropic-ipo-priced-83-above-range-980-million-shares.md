Anthropic has priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the range it set at the start of its roadshow. "We've priced our IPO at $83 per share, with trading expected to begin on Nasdaq tomorrow," the company [posted on X](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2123196808675405860) on Wednesday evening.

## The final terms

According to Anthropic's [pricing announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering), the offering is 980,000,000 shares of Class A common stock. Anthropic is selling 956,351,354 of them and existing stockholders are selling 23,648,646. Anthropic receives nothing from the shares sold by those stockholders.

The shares are expected to start trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market today, 19 November, under the symbol ANT. The deal is expected to close on 20 November. The underwriters have a 30-day option to buy up to 147,000,000 more shares. The Securities and Exchange Commission declared the registration statement effective on 18 November.

## How it changed since the roadshow

When Anthropic [launched its roadshow](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch) on 10 November, it planned to sell 825,000,000 shares at $70 to $78, with 801,351,354 coming from the company. So the final deal is bigger on both counts: the price is $5 above the top of the range and the company is selling about 155 million more shares itself. The number sold by existing holders did not change. The underwriters' option also grew, from 123,750,000 shares to 147,000,000.

By our arithmetic, 980 million shares at $83 comes to about $81.3 billion, of which about $79.4 billion is for the shares Anthropic itself is selling, before underwriting fees and expenses. If the underwriters take up their full option, that adds about $12.2 billion. Anthropic has not published its net proceeds in the announcement.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are named as the contacts for copies of the final prospectus.

## What to watch today

The price says demand was strong enough for Anthropic to raise both the price and the size. The first trade on Nasdaq will show whether that demand carries over into the open market.
