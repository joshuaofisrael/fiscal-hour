The 30-year fixed mortgage in Freddie Mac’s survey averaged 7.28% as of 19 November 2026, the company said in its [Primary Mortgage Market Survey release](https://freddiemac.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/mortgage-rates-average-728-0/). A week earlier the average was 7.11%. A year earlier it was 6.26%. The 15-year average was 6.66%, up from 6.45% the week before and from 5.54% a year earlier. This page started as the 29 October report, when the 30-year average was 7.24%. The weeks since are below, from Freddie Mac’s own archive, checked 20 November 2026.

The survey is a weekly average of a defined loan. It is not a rate a particular borrower will be offered, and it is not a suggestion to buy a house or to refinance. The next publication date on [Freddie Mac’s calendar](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms/docs/PMMS_Publication_Calendar.pdf) is 25 November 2026. That date is also on the [release calendar](/calendar/).

## What the survey measures

Freddie Mac says the survey covers conventional, conforming, fully amortizing home-purchase loans for borrowers who put 20% down and have excellent credit. The [PMMS page](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) says the figures come from loan applications submitted through Loan Product Advisor, not from a phone survey of lenders. The week of applications runs from 12:00 a.m. ET on the prior Thursday through 11:59 p.m. ET on Wednesday. Results are released at 12 p.m. ET on Thursday, or on Wednesday when a U.S. holiday falls on Thursday. Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday 26 November, which is why the next date is a Wednesday.

The same page says adjustable-rate averages and average fees and points are no longer published. Points dropped out because, under the current application data, lenders are not always required to report them. That is a gap in the series: the 7.28% figure is a rate, and the release does not say how many points sat on top of it.

## Eight weeks, back where October started

The [2026 archive](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms/archive) lists both the 30-year and 15-year averages for each publication date. From 1 October through 19 November the 30-year reading fell for four straight weeks and then rose. The 19 November print, 7.28%, matches the 1 October print. The low in that stretch was 12 November, at 7.11%.

| 1 Oct 2026 | 7.28% | 6.60% |
| 8 Oct 2026 | 7.40% | 6.73% |
| 15 Oct 2026 | 7.40% | 6.71% |
| 22 Oct 2026 | 7.32% | 6.68% |
| 29 Oct 2026 | 7.24% | 6.60% |
| 5 Nov 2026 | 7.18% | 6.57% |
| 12 Nov 2026 | 7.11% | 6.45% |
| 19 Nov 2026 | 7.28% | 6.66% |

The 29 October release, the one this story first reported, put the 30-year average at 7.24%, down from 7.32% the week before and up from 6.17% a year earlier. The 15-year average that week was 6.60%, down from 6.68% and up from 5.41% a year earlier. Those year-ago comparisons are in that [29 October release](https://freddiemac.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/mortgage-rates-decline-further-724/). The archive table above does not repeat a year-ago column for the other weeks, and this story does not invent them.

Sam Khater, the company’s chief economist, said in the 29 October release that the 30-year average had declined again, reached its lowest level that month, and modestly boosted purchasing power for prospective buyers. “Lowest level this month” and the purchasing-power comment are his. In the 19 November release he talked about borrowers comparing more than one mortgage quote. That is his comment. It is not a recommendation from FiscalHour, and neither release says what a monthly payment will be on a given house.

## Where the 7% line shows up elsewhere

A survey average and a builder’s complaint are different measurements. The [NAHB/Wells Fargo index](/articles/2026-11-18-nahb-builder-confidence-november-2026-lowest-since-2012/) for November, released 17 November, quoted the association’s chief economist saying mortgage rates above 7% were holding buyers back. Two days later the Freddie Mac average was 7.28%. The [Michigan homebuying survey](/articles/2026-10-26-michigan-survey-homebuying/) and the [Case-Shiller price index](/articles/2026-10-28-case-shiller-august-home-prices/) describe other parts of the same market. None of them is a rate a lender has offered a named borrower.
