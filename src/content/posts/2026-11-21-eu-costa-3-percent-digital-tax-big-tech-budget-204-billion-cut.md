The European Union wants the world's biggest tech companies to help pay for its next budget. "EU's Costa Brings Back Digital Tax Plan in Push to Secure Agreement on Bloc's Budget," [First Squawk posted on X](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124105937996943559) on Saturday morning, citing the Financial Times.

## What Costa proposed

European Council President António Costa set out a new draft of the bloc's 2028-2034 long-term budget on Saturday. In his [statement](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/21/multiannual-financial-framework-president-costa-s-proposal/), he said the plan is 10% lower than the European Commission's original proposal, a cut of €204 billion in current prices, and that it includes "a balanced package of 5 revenue streams." The first one on his list is revenue "from the digital services revenues of large companies."

According to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/21/eu-budget-costa-pitches-more-cuts-big-tech-tax-to-break-deadlock) and [AFP](https://www.afp.com/en/eu-proposes-tax-big-tech-boost-budget), the levy would be 3% of annual net turnover from online advertising, digital intermediation services and user data. It would apply to companies with at least €50 million of digital turnover in the EU and more than €750 million worldwide. It replaces CORE, a charge on large companies that the Commission had proposed and most governments opposed.

The other new revenue streams are the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, non-recycled electronic waste, tobacco products and the carbon Emissions Trading System. Costa said that, together with existing sources, they "can generate about 60 billion euros in revenue each year." Euronews says the Commission had estimated the digital levy itself at about €5 billion a year.

## The pushback has already started

Costa said the package keeps national contributions close to today's level, rising on average from 0.80% to 0.82% of gross national income. "I believe this proposal can bring us closer to a final agreement," he said.

Paris is not convinced. "The new negotiating framework for the next multiannual European budget is very disappointing," a source from the Élysée told reporters, according to Euronews. "As it stands, this proposal from the President of the European Council does not constitute a basis for an agreement."

## Why markets should care

Most companies big enough to pay the levy are American. AFP notes the plan is likely to anger the Trump administration, which has reacted sharply to EU moves against Big Tech. "This tax is in no way directed against any particular company or country," Costa said, according to AFP. Euronews reports that fear of US trade retaliation is the main source of opposition.

The tax is only a proposal. EU leaders meet on Thursday, and the budget needs every member state on board and then the European Parliament. Investors in US megacaps should watch whether the levy survives that summit and how Washington responds.
