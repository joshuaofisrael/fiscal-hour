Berkshire Hathaway bought back about $9.6 billion of its own stock in the third quarter, more than double the $4.5 billion it repurchased in the second quarter, even as operating earnings fell, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/berkshire-hathaway-q3-2026-earnings-buybacks.html) from the company's results released on Saturday, 7 November. Buybacks for 2026 now total about $14.4 billion.

## Insurance drags on operating profit

Operating earnings, which cover Berkshire's wholly owned businesses, fell 5.8% to $12.705 billion from $13.485 billion a year earlier. Insurance underwriting profit dropped 43% to $1.352 billion and insurance investment income fell 14% to $2.734 billion. The railroad, energy and manufacturing businesses all grew earnings year on year, CNBC said.

## Net earnings and investment gains

Net earnings, which include gains and losses on Berkshire's large stock portfolio, fell 28% to $22.112 billion from $30.796 billion. Investment gains roughly halved to $9.407 billion. Berkshire has long cautioned that quarterly swings in investment values make net earnings a poor guide to its underlying operations.

## Cash pile

Cash and equivalents rose 3% in the quarter to $377.435 billion from $365.514 billion. Excluding rail and utility cash and Treasury bill payables, the figure was $352.3 billion, down 1.9% from the second quarter. Berkshire's five largest equity holdings were unchanged: Alphabet, American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola.

CNBC noted that Berkshire class A shares were up more than 12% over the past month, while the S&P 500 fell 5.5% over the same period. This is news coverage, not investment advice.
