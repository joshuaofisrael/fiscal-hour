The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 572.25 points, or 1.09%, to 51,770.31 on Friday, 30 October. The S&P 500 lost 140.32 points, or 1.82%, to 7,580.07, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 747.73 points, or 2.86%, to 25,437.00, [Reuters reported](https://www.streetinsider.com/Market+Check/Wall+Street+closes+sharply+lower+as+AI+cash+burn+fears+spark+tech%C2%A0selloff/27260146.html). Reuters said it was the Nasdaq's largest one-day percentage drop since 5 June, and that the index had fallen 7.8% from its closing high on 6 October.

Reuters linked the fall to quarterly results from Intel and Amazon released late on Thursday, which it said added to worries about how much AI-related companies are spending on the technology. Intel shares fell 28.6%, Nvidia fell 4.2% and AMD fell 10.9%, Reuters reported, while the broader chip index lost 7.2%. [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-stocks-lower-at-close-of-trade-dow-jones-industrial-average-down-109-4988649) listed Intel, Qualcomm and AMD among the S&P 500's weakest stocks of the session. FiscalHour covered [Intel's outlook](/articles/2026-10-30-intel-fourth-quarter-outlook/) on Friday.

Not every sector fell. Reuters said ExxonMobil and Chevron rose 2.2% and 2.8% after both beat profit expectations, while Coinbase, Strategy and Robinhood fell between 7.2% and 10.6% after Binance reported a large security incident. Investing.com listed FMC, Regeneron and Global Payments among the best performers in the S&P 500. Declining stocks outnumbered advancing ones by about 1.8 to 1 on the New York Stock Exchange, Reuters said.

Oliver Pursche of Wealthspire Advisors told Reuters the week had been about "the future of AI" and whether the spending will earn a positive return. That is his view of the week, not a forecast. Reuters said the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq ended October with monthly losses, while the Dow ended the month higher. According to LSEG data cited by Reuters, 82% of the 299 S&P 500 companies that had reported third-quarter results by Friday had beaten analysts' estimates.
