For most of the past year, copper flowed into the United States as traders raced to beat a possible import tariff. Now some of it is heading back out. "US Copper Flows to China Resume as Concerns Over Trump Tariffs Ease," [First Squawk posted on X](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124120802270580917) on Saturday, citing the Financial Times.

## How much is moving

BMO Capital Markets estimates that traders have booked 30,000 to 100,000 tonnes of refined copper to ship from the US West Coast to China, [The Northern Miner reports](https://www.northernminer.com/news/us-copper-booked-for-china-as-tariff-bet-unwinds/1003898084/). "Copper units are now starting to leave the U.S. west coast destined for China, an event we had assigned a very low probability to," BMO analysts Helen Amos and George Heppel wrote. They estimate the voyage takes about two weeks.

[Business Upturn](https://businessupturn.com/usa/news/us-china-copper-trade-takes-an-unexpected-turn-as-tariff-fears-fade-and-chinese-demand-strengthens/), summarising the FT report, says shipments are being arranged from ports including Long Beach, New Orleans and Baltimore, and that much larger volumes could reach China by February if the trend continues.

## Why the trade flipped

The draw is price. China's Yangshan premium, the extra cost of importing copper into bonded warehouses, hit a record $500 a tonne above London prices on Thursday, according to The Northern Miner. [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/copper-price-sinks-into-bear-market-erasing-2026-gains-in-worst-week-of-the-year/), citing Reuters, says it slipped to $495 on Friday, up from $360 on Wednesday and less than $100 in September. Shanghai Futures Exchange stocks of 30,846 tonnes are still less than a day of Chinese consumption, it adds.

The tariff threat has also faded in traders' minds. A July 2025 White House proclamation put duties on semi-finished copper products and called for a review of refined-copper duties of 15% from 1 January 2027 and 30% from 2028. BMO says traders it spoke to in China mostly expect that refined duty to be dropped, while Western traders still lean towards a tariff. Washington has not said the tariff is off.

## The price is falling anyway

None of this has helped the futures price. MINING.COM says copper slid into a bear market on Friday, ten weeks after a record high, as investors pulled back from AI-linked trades and the dollar strengthened. Three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange traded at $12,310.50 a tonne early Friday, about 8.2% lower on the week, The Northern Miner reports, citing Reuters.

BMO says copper could fall towards $10,000 to $11,000 a tonne without a signal from Washington, though its medium-term view stays bullish. The next thing to watch is a decision on the refined-copper tariff.
