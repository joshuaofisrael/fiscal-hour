US industrial production fell 0.4% in October after rising 0.7% in September, the Federal Reserve said on Tuesday. The Fed [posted the headline figures on X](https://x.com/federalreserve/status/2122695250812584194): total output down 0.4%, manufacturing up 0.3%, utilities down 4.3% and mining down 0.8%, with capacity utilisation at 76.4%.

## Where the drop came from

According to the [G.17 release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/20261117/default.htm), the decline was driven by utilities and mining rather than factories. Utility output fell 4.3% after rising 4.4% in September, with both electric and natural gas utilities lower. Mining output dropped 0.8%.

Manufacturing rose 0.3%. Durable goods output climbed 0.5%, led by a 4.4% rise in motor vehicles and parts, while nondurable manufacturing was unchanged. Excluding motor vehicles and parts, factory output edged down 0.1%, so the gain was narrow.

At 103.4% of its 2017 average, total industrial production was 2.1% higher than a year earlier.

## Capacity use

Capacity utilisation for all industry fell to 76.4%, which the Fed says is 3.0 percentage points below its 1972 to 2025 average. Manufacturing utilisation ticked up to 76.0%, still 2.2 points below its long-run average. Mining's operating rate fell 0.8 point to 86.4%, above its average, while utilities fell 3.3 points to 70.2%.

## By market group

Business equipment output rose 1.0% and defence and space equipment 0.9%, but every other major market group declined. Consumer goods production fell 0.7%, as gains in automotive products were outweighed by lower non-energy nondurables and energy. Materials output dropped 0.7%, pulled down by a 2.2% fall in its energy component.

## What the release does not say

The Fed’s release says utility output fell after rising the month before, and it puts capacity use 3.0 points under the 1972 to 2025 average. That comparison is the Fed’s. The G.17 does not say whether the next GDP print will be weak, and this story does not add that claim. Factory output outside motor vehicles slipped 0.1 percent in the same release. That is a narrow monthly move, not a verdict on the expansion.
