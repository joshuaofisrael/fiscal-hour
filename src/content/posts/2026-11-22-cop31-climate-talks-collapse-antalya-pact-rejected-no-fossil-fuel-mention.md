The world's annual climate summit has ended without the deal it was built around. "UN Climate Talks Slide Toward Collapse as Nations Reject Deal," [Bloomberg posted on X](https://x.com/business/status/2124476784674505146) on Sunday morning as talks in Antalya, Turkey, ran past their scheduled close.

## What was rejected

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/nov/22/cop31-climate-talks-set-to-end-in-deep-division-as-key-deal-rejected), the European Union, the UK and developing-country allies refused to back the "Antalya pact", the main decision text drafted by the Turkish and Australian presidencies. The draft invited countries to speed up electrification and finance and to limit overshoot of the 1.5C warming goal. It did not mention fossil fuels and did not endorse the "transition away from fossil fuels" that, the Guardian says, more than 130 countries had pushed for. It did include carbon capture and storage.

Speaking for the EU, Ireland's Darragh O'Brien said the text "failed to meet the moment". "We will not endorse a climate decision that does not even mention fossil fuels," he said, according to the Guardian.

The Guardian reports that Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway also objected, while Saudi Arabia defended the role of carbon capture. COP31 president Murat Kurum accepted there was no consensus. "We recognise that there is no consensus on this document. Therefore, these decisions have not been adopted," he said, according to the Guardian. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/climate-un-turkey-fossil-fuels-global-warming-f6e36c4caf13ec6fed57c9f077cbd067) reported that the talks broke down without an overarching agreement.

## Why markets should care

Climate summits do not set laws, but they shape the direction of energy policy that governments then turn into rules, subsidies and taxes. A summit that ends without agreed language on fossil fuels leaves oil and gas producers without new pressure from the UN process, and leaves clean-energy investors without the stronger signal many had hoped for.

The split also matters. Europe drew a line over fossil-fuel language, while producers defended carbon capture. Expect that divide to show up in EU climate rules and in trade talks with energy exporters in 2027.

## What happens next

The Guardian reports that the presidencies avoided a total collapse by passing decisions on related issues, including the "just transition" for workers in high-carbon industries. The bigger fight over fossil fuels moves to national policy and to next year's summit. This is economic news, not investment advice.
