Stripe has closed its purchase of Parafin, the San Francisco company that lets software platforms offer financing to the small businesses that use them. An update dated 11 November on [Parafin's announcement page](https://www.parafin.com/blog/parafin-is-joining-stripe) says "the acquisition closed today, and Parafin is now part of Stripe." Stripe marked the moment the same day in a [post on X](https://x.com/stripe/status/2120550087256682585), saying the two will work together to expand revenue opportunities for platforms and help small businesses grow.

## What Stripe bought

Stripe first announced the agreement on 30 September. Its [newsroom release](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-agrees-to-acquire-parafin) described Parafin as an embedded financial products platform that provides credit offerings to the small business customers of platforms including DoorDash, Gusto, Jobber and Mindbody. Stripe said more than 18,000 platforms build on its own infrastructure and that demand for its Stripe Capital product had risen as new business formation picked up.

Parafin's founders wrote that the company has funded more than $3 billion to over 60,000 small businesses in the US since its first cash advance in 2021. Its product range now covers flexible and term loans, business-to-business pay-over-time and credit cards, all offered under the partner platform's own brand. The founders said existing offers, outstanding financing and repayment terms for partners and their customers are unaffected by the deal.

## Price not disclosed

Neither company has published a purchase price. [Payments Dive reported](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/stripe-swallows-parafin/831903/) at the time of the announcement that a Stripe spokesperson declined to comment on the price, and noted Parafin had been valued at $750 million after a December 2024 funding round. The outlet also said the deal was Stripe's second acquisition in two months, following its purchase of AI routing company OpenRouter in August.

## Analysis

The completed deal puts a dedicated small-business credit engine inside one of the largest payment processors serving software platforms. Stripe has said it wants to offer a wider range of credit products to more of those platforms; how quickly Parafin's products are folded into Stripe Capital, and under which brand, has not yet been set out publicly.
