The Securities and Exchange Commission has proposed a wide rewrite of the rules that govern how US public companies disclose what they pay their executives and directors. The SEC said on its [official X account](https://x.com/SECGov/status/2121294677202640944) on Friday that the amendments aim to make the requirements "more principles-based and materiality-driven", and published the details in a [press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-114-sec-proposes-executive-director-compensation-disclosure-reforms).

If adopted, it would be the most significant change to executive pay disclosure since the 2006 overhaul that introduced the Compensation Discussion and Analysis, according to a summary by law firm [Proskauer](https://www.erisapracticecenter.com/2026/11/sec-proposes-sweeping-overhaul-of-executive-compensation-disclosure-rules/), which describes the release as 542 pages long.

## Fewer named executives

Companies typically disclose pay for five or more named executive officers today. The proposal would limit that to the principal executive officer and principal financial officer, usually the CEO and CFO, [Equilar reports](https://www.equilar.com/blogs/646-sec-exec-comp-disclosure-proposal.html).

## Three new tables

The Summary Compensation Table and the other main tables would be replaced by three new ones, Equilar says: a Granted Compensation Table showing what a company awarded for the year, an Earned Compensation Table showing what was actually earned, and a Status of Equity Awards Table that tracks each award from grant until it is earned or expires.

## Other changes

- **Perquisites:** the reporting threshold would rise from $10,000 to $150,000 per named executive, with an exclusion for de minimis fringe benefits, according to Equilar and Proskauer.
- **Pay versus performance:** the disclosure would stay but be simplified, using cumulative total shareholder return as the required measure, three years of data instead of five, and only the CEO and CFO, Equilar says.
- **CEO pay ratio:** the median employee would be drawn only from US full-time staff and identified every five years instead of every three.
- **CD&A:** the narrative would have to cover three topics, replacing a list of 15 examples.

In a [statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-proposing-release-executive-compensation-disclosure-reform-111326), SEC Chairman Paul Atkins said the Commission last comprehensively reviewed the pay disclosure rule, Item 402, twenty years ago with the 2006 amendments.

## What happens next

Equilar says the comment period will stay open for 60 days after the proposal is published in the Federal Register. Nothing changes unless the SEC adopts a final rule.

## Analysis

The proposal would shrink what companies must report, which could cut compliance work for issuers. Investors who use the detailed tables and perquisite disclosures to judge pay may push back during the comment period, so the final rule could look different.
